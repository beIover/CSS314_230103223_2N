### 2. Strong Scaling Results

| Processes | N | Stage A (ms) | Total (ms) | Speedup | Efficiency |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1,200,000 | 47.131 | 2024.037 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 2 | 1,200,000 | 47.113 | 1090.845 | 1.855 | 0.928 |
| 3 | 1,200,000 | 46.926 | 712.173 | 2.842 | 0.947 |
| 4 | 1,200,000 | 46.807 | 559.302 | 3.619 | 0.905 |
| 5 | 1,200,000 | 47.752 | 563.847 | 3.590 | 0.718 |
| 6 | 1,200,000 | 47.422 | 571.777 | 3.540 | 0.590 |
| 7 | 1,200,000 | 53.324 | 614.871 | 3.292 | 0.470 |
| 8 | 1,200,000 | 54.446 | 609.092 | 3.323 | 0.415 |

**Analysis:**

Strong scaling improved significantly as the process count increased from 1 to 4. The best execution time was 559.302 ms at 4 processes, with a speedup of 3.619× and efficiency of 0.905.

Adding a fifth process did not improve performance. Execution time increased slightly to 563.847 ms. Performance also remained below the four-process result at 6, 7, and 8 processes.

This behavior is consistent with the hardware configuration: the CPU has 4 physical cores and 8 logical cores. Using more than 4 processes can introduce additional scheduling overhead and resource contention.

The marginal speedup gain from 3 to 4 processes was:

- Speedup gain: 3.619 − 2.842 = 0.777

From 4 to 5 processes:

- Speedup change: 3.590 − 3.619 = −0.029

Therefore, the fifth process produced a small performance regression rather than an improvement.

### 3. Amdahl's Law

Measured serial fraction:

- s = 0.023286

Theoretical maximum speedup:

- 1 / s = 42.9445

Observed speedup at 8 logical processes:

- 3.3230

The theoretical limit assumes ideal parallel execution apart from the serial fraction. Actual performance is lower because of multiprocessing overhead, scheduling, and hardware resource contention.

**Analysis:**

Weak scaling kept execution time relatively stable from 1 to 4 processes, even though the problem size increased proportionally with the number of processes.

At 5 processes and above, execution time increased more noticeably. This suggests that the system was approaching or exceeding its most effective parallel capacity.

Efficiency values above 1 at 2 and 3 processes indicate superlinear measured performance. This can occur due to measurement variation and fixed overheads in the baseline; it does not mean that the CPU performs more than 100% of its theoretical capacity.

### 5. Gustafson's Law

At 8 logical processes:

- Measured serial fraction for the scaled run: α = 0.0898 (approximately)
- Gustafson scaled speedup: 7.3715

Gustafson's Law demonstrates that parallel systems can handle larger workloads efficiently when the problem size grows with the number of processors. In this experiment, the measured speedup was 7.3715 at 8 processes.

### 6. Architectural Analysis

- Physical cores: 4
